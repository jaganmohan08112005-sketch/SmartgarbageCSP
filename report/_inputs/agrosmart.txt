ABSTRACT
Agriculture depends strongly on timely irrigation, suitable soil conditions, and changing weather conditions. Farmers may have access to soil-moisture information but can face difficulty in converting soil and weather conditions into clear irrigation decisions. AgroMind Smart Farming is a web-based smart irrigation and farm advisory system developed to provide simple and practical irrigation guidance. The system considers factors such as crop type, soil type, farm size, location, water availability, and soil moisture, along with weather conditions. This approach is consistent with established irrigation-planning methods that consider crop, soil, and climate information.
The system allows farmers to create and manage farm profiles and enter soil-moisture values directly or provide multiple readings, which are averaged to obtain a representative value. Weather information such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, and rain probability is obtained automatically using the Open-Meteo service. The Irrigation Planner analyzes the available information using predefined decision rules and generates irrigation status, recommended time, duration, estimated water requirement, risk level, and water-saving suggestions. The Dashboard, Weather, Crop Health, and Analytics modules provide a clear view of the farm condition and historical information.
AgroMind also includes an AI Assistant that uses the available farm context to explain irrigation results and answer natural-language farming questions. The system is developed using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Flask, PostgreSQL, Open-Meteo, and AI-assisted services. The overall workflow consists of collecting farm information, obtaining weather data, processing soil-moisture readings, analyzing the conditions, generating an irrigation recommendation, and providing an understandable explanation to the farmer. The proposed system aims to support practical irrigation planning and promote more informed water-management decisions through an accessible web-based platform.

1. INTRODUCTION
Agriculture is an important sector that depends heavily on effective water management. Proper irrigation requires consideration of factors such as crop type, soil characteristics, soil moisture, weather conditions, and water availability. Tools such as FAO CROPWAT demonstrate the importance of using soil, climate, and crop information for irrigation planning and scheduling.
Small and community farmers may often depend on fixed schedules or personal experience to decide when to irrigate their crops. These methods may not always account for changing soil-moisture and weather conditions, which can result in over-irrigation, under-irrigation, or unnecessary water usage. Therefore, there is a need for a simple and accessible system that can combine farm information, manual soil-moisture observations, and weather data to support better irrigation decisions.
AgroMind Smart Farming is a web-based agricultural decision-support system developed to provide simple and understandable irrigation guidance. The system uses farm details, soil-moisture readings, and automatically obtained weather information to analyze irrigation requirements using predefined rules. It also provides Dashboard, Irrigation Planner, Weather, Crop Health, Analytics, and AI Assistant modules. The Generative AI assistant helps explain recommendations and answer farming-related questions, while the system is designed to work without requiring permanent IoT infrastructure.
1.1 Problem Statement
Small and community farmers often face difficulty in deciding when to irrigate and how much water to use. Irrigation decisions based mainly on fixed schedules or personal experience may not sufficiently consider soil moisture, crop requirements, and changing weather conditions, which can lead to water wastage or inadequate irrigation. Established irrigation approaches also consider crop, soil, and climate conditions for scheduling.
Many advanced smart-farming solutions depend on sensors, IoT devices, or complex infrastructure, which may not be suitable for every small-scale farming environment. Therefore, there is a need for a simple, affordable, and user-friendly web-based system that can combine farm information, manual soil-moisture readings, and weather data to provide understandable irrigation guidance.

2. LITERATURE SURVEY
2.1 Crop Water Requirement and Irrigation Scheduling
Irrigation scheduling is an important part of agricultural water management. The irrigation requirement of a crop depends on factors such as crop characteristics, soil conditions, weather, rainfall, and available soil water. FAO CROPWAT is a decision-support tool that calculates crop-water and irrigation requirements using soil, climate, and crop data and can develop irrigation schedules based on soil-water balance.
2.2 Agricultural Decision Support Systems
Agricultural Decision Support Systems (DSS) help farmers and agricultural professionals make decisions by combining different types of agricultural information. Models such as FAO AquaCrop use weather data, crop characteristics, soil characteristics, and management practices to study crop response to water and support irrigation and water-management decisions.
2.3 Weather and Soil Information for Irrigation
Weather and soil conditions play an important role in determining irrigation requirements. Temperature, rainfall, and other climatic variables influence crop water use, while soil characteristics affect the amount of water available to crops. Recent irrigation-scheduling guidance also emphasizes considering crop water requirements, soil-water relationships, climate, and crop growth stages when planning irrigation
2.4 AI and Machine Learning in Agriculture
Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning techniques are increasingly used in agriculture for applications such as crop monitoring, disease detection, yield prediction, and irrigation management. AI-based systems can process historical and environmental data to identify patterns and provide recommendations. In addition, AI assistants can present agricultural information in a simpler and more interactive form for users.
2.5 Research Gap
Existing agricultural decision-support approaches show that effective irrigation planning requires the consideration of crop, soil, weather, and water-related information. However, many existing tools are designed for detailed agricultural modelling and may require specialized knowledge to operation

This keeps the prototype's core recommendation path understandable and easy to demonstrate.

4. METHODOLOGY / SYSTEM DESIGN
The methodology of AgroMind Smart Farming follows a step-by-step process in which farm information, soil-moisture readings, and weather conditions are collected and processed to provide irrigation guidance.
1.User Login and Farm Selection
The farmer first logs into the system and creates or selects a farm profile. The farm profile contains important information such as farm name, location, crop type, soil type, farm size, and water availability.
2. Soil-Moisture Collection
The farmer provides the soil-moisture information. If a reliable value is already available, it can be entered directly. If multiple readings are taken from different locations in the farm, the system calculates their average to obtain a representative soil-moisture value.
3. Weather Data Collection
The system obtains weather information automatically based on the selected farm location. AgroMind uses the Open-Meteo Weather API, which provides variables such as temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, and precipitation probability.
4.Data Processing and Analysis
The collected farm information, soil-moisture value, and weather conditions are passed to the irrigation decision engine. The system checks these inputs against the predefined rules used by the project.
5.Irrigation Decision
The decision engine determines whether irrigation is required based on the available conditions. If irrigation is recommended, the system provides information such as irrigation status, suggested time, duration, estimated water quantity, risk level, and water-saving suggestions.
6.AI-Assisted Explanation
The AI Assistant uses the available farm context and the generated result to provide a simple explanation. It can also answer natural-language farming questions and provide general guidance.
15

7.Storage and Monitoring
The farm information and analysis results are stored in the PostgreSQL database. Historical records are used by the Analytics module to help the farmer monitor changes in soil moisture, weather conditions, and irrigation activities.
8.Dashboard Output
The final information is presented through the Dashboard and other modules such as Irrigation Planner, Weather, Crop Health, Analytics, and AI Assistant.

4.1 System Design
The System Flow begins with the farmer logging in and creating or selecting a farm profile. The farmer then enters crop, soil, farm size, location, and water availability, followed by soil moisture readings. The system fetches real-time weather data using the Open-Meteo API and analyzes the farm, soil, and weather conditions. Based on this analysis, the system generates irrigation recommendations, including timing, water quantity, and risk level. Finally, the AI Assistant provides explanations and answers, while the dashboard displays all relevant farm details and recommendations.

5. IMPLEMENTATION / MODULES
5.1 Implementation
AgroMind Smart Farming is implemented as a web-based agricultural decision-support system. The frontend is developed using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, while Flask/Python is used for backend processing. PostgreSQL is used to store user, farm, analysis, and historical information.
The implementation follows a modular approach in which each module performs a specific function and communicates with the backend when required. Weather information is obtained from Open-Meteo using the farm's location. Open-Meteo provides current and forecast weather variables including temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, and precipitation probability.
The irrigation analysis is performed by a rule-based decision engine. It receives farm details, soil-moisture information, and weather conditions as inputs and generates an irrigation recommendation. The AI Assistant is used separately to explain the generated results and answer farming-related questions.

7. IMPACT ASSESSMENT
7.1 Farmer Impact
AgroMind is intended to reduce the effort required to interpret several farm conditions at once. Instead of manually comparing soil moisture, weather, crop, soil, and water availability, the farmer receives a consolidated recommendation. The simple dashboard and dedicated planner can improve accessibility for users who prefer clear actions over complex analytics.
7.2 Water-Use Impact
The system emphasizes irrigation only when the analyzed conditions indicate a need. Weather-aware recommendations can help avoid unnecessary watering when rainfall is likely, while water-saving advice encourages appropriate timing and controlled use. The project does not claim a measured percentage of water savings without field validation; the water-saving value should be evaluated using real farm records.
7.3 Decision-Making Impact
A transparent decision path can improve the farmer's understanding of why a recommendation was generated. The planner exposes the main inputs and the final decision, while Analytics allows the farmer to compare current conditions with historical records. This can support more consistent planning than relying only on memory or fixed schedules.
7.4 Educational Impact
The project demonstrates how web development, databases, external APIs, decision logic, data visualization, and AI can be combined to solve a practical agricultural problem. It also provides a foundation for future student work involving real sensors, validated agronomic models, and field testing.
7.5 Operational Feasibility
AgroMind is designed as a browser-based application, which reduces the hardware requirements for the prototype. A farmer can interact with the system through a web interface, while weather information is retrieved automatically. The architecture separates external services from the decision logic, making it possible to replace or extend components later.
7.6 Environmental Relevance
Efficient irrigation can contribute to responsible water use when recommendations are accurate and followed appropriately. AgroMind supports this objective by considering rainfall and soil moisture before suggesting irrigation. The project does not claim direct environmental
29

measurements; its contribution at prototype stage is the provision of a water-aware decision-support workflow.
7.7 Scalability
The modular design can be extended from a single farm profile to multiple farms and users. Additional crops, soil categories, validated moisture references, richer weather variables, and sensor inputs can be added without changing the basic navigation structure. The Flask backend and PostgreSQL prototype can also be replaced or scaled with a larger relational database when deployment requirements increase.
7.8 Limitations of Impact Assessment
The current project is a prototype and its impact should be treated as expected or potential impact rather than measured field performance. Real-world validation requires long-term farm observations, reliable sensor measurements, crop-specific reference data, comparison with existing irrigation practice, and assessment across different seasons and locations.

8.1 Usability Challenge
One of the main design challenges is balancing technical detail with simplicity. A farmer may not want to interpret several charts before deciding whether to irrigate. AgroMind therefore uses progressive detail: the Dashboard provides the summary, the Planner provides the calculation and recommendation, Weather provides detailed forecast information, and Analytics provides historical evidence.
8.2 Data Quality Challenge
The quality of an irrigation recommendation depends on the quality of soil-moisture readings and other inputs. The multiple-reading feature helps the farmer obtain a representative average, but averaging cannot correct an incorrect or poorly collected measurement. Future versions should support validated sensor readings and field calibration.

9. CONCLUSION
AgroMind is a web-based smart irrigation and farm advisory system designed to help farmers understand current farm conditions and make informed irrigation decisions. It integrates a farm profile, current weather information, soil-moisture input, irrigation decision logic, crop health summary, historical analytics, and a farm-aware AI Assistant into a single platform. The central workflow involves entering farm details, selecting a location, fetching weather data, entering soil-moisture values directly or calculating them from multiple readings, analyzing the combined conditions through the decision engine, and generating a clear irrigation recommendation, while AI provides a natural-language explanation of the result. The proposed website structure addresses the major objectives of the project by providing a farmer profile, dashboard, weather module, irrigation planner, crop health module, analytics, and AI Assistant, with a modular design that connects these features into one integrated workflow rather than isolated components. However, the system has certain limitations: its recommendations are decision-support outputs that require real-world validation; weather and AI functionality depend on external services and network availability; soil-moisture accuracy depends on the quality of farmer-provided readings or future sensor input; the prototype does not directly control physical irrigation equipment; and the actual impact and water-saving benefits should be measured through field trials rather than assumed.

10. FUTURE WORK
10.1 Real-Time Soil Sensors
Future versions can integrate soil-moisture sensors so that readings are collected automatically instead of being entered manually. Multiple sensor points could provide spatial information and reduce dependence on a single measurement.
10.2 Automated Irrigation Hardware
After sufficient field validation, AgroMind could be connected to pumps, valves, or irrigation controllers. Such integration should use safety limits, manual override, sensor validation, and fail-safe behavior so that a software recommendation cannot unintentionally cause excessive watering.
10.3 Advanced Crop Models
Crop-specific and soil-specific reference datasets can be expanded and validated with agricultural expertise. More detailed models could consider crop growth stage, evapotranspiration, root-zone conditions, and irrigation method.
10.4 Improved Weather Intelligence
Future work can extend the weather module with longer forecasts, additional precipitation variables, severe-weather alerts, and localized farming advisories. Historical weather can also be combined with irrigation records for improved planning.
10.5 Advanced Analytics
Analytics can be extended with predictive trends, comparative water-use reports, farm-to-farm benchmarking, seasonal summaries, and exportable reports. These features can help farmers evaluate whether irrigation practices are becoming more efficient.
10.6 AI Improvements
The AI Assistant can be expanded with retrieval from validated agricultural knowledge sources, multilingual interaction including Telugu, voice input, speech responses, and stronger grounding controls.
10.7 Deployment and Scalability
Future deployment can migrate from a prototype PostgreSQL database to a production relational database, add stronger authentication, monitoring, backups, role-based access, and secure configuration. A responsive mobile-first interface or dedicated application can also be considered.

The AI Assistant receives only the information required to generate a relevant response, such as:

API keys and other sensitive credentials are kept server-side using environment variables rather than being exposed in frontend code. AI's documentation also recommends configuring the API key through an environment variable.
This structure keeps the decision engine responsible for irrigation analysis, while the AI Assistant is used mainly for explanation and natural-language interaction.
